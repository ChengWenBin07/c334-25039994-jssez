03 / Plan with care

Incentives & tax watch-outs

The Playbook describes incentives and highlights cross-border tax and compliance considerations.

Incentives named in the Playbook

  • Corporate tax: The overview lists a 5% rate for 10 or 15 years for qualifying manufacturing activities. It lists capital-investment thresholds (excluding land) of RM500 million and RM1 billion.
  • Knowledge workers: The overview lists a 5% personal income tax rate.
  • Sector-specific support: The Playbook mentions incentives, grants and facilitation support from both sides. It describes Global Services Hub tax treatment as tied to operational scale and workforce quality, and Smart Logistics investment allowances.
Playbook, pp. 3, 10. Thresholds and eligibility apply as described in the Playbook.
01

Permanent establishment

Frequent Johor travel for supervision, negotiations or business decisions may create a taxable presence. The Playbook advises clearly separating activities and keeping records.

Playbook, p. 14
02

Transfer pricing

Related-party prices should follow the arm’s length principle. Define each entity’s responsibilities and keep supporting documentation.

Playbook, p. 14
03

Incentive conditions

Singapore and Malaysia schemes have different requirements. The Playbook says incentives should follow the operating model, not dictate it.

Playbook, p. 15
04

Cross-border workdays

Work in Johor may create personal tax and immigration obligations. Track workdays, locations and activities; treaty relief depends on specific conditions.

Playbook, p. 16

Summaries from the Playbook, not individual tax or legal advice. Check eligibility and requirements with the agencies named in the Playbook.

04 / People and mobility

Passes for staff

The Playbook’s appendix summarises expatriate Employment Pass applications, Professional Visit Passes and Investor Passes.

Playbook, pp. 28–29

Employment Pass

Expatriate posts

The Playbook says MIDA evaluates expatriate-post applications for manufacturing, selected service sectors and approved Regional Establishments or Regional Offices. It describes:

  • Key Post: a permanent, essential high-level managerial post.
  • Term Post: a temporary post for a specific period, requiring relevant qualifications, experience or expertise.

EP salary thresholds listed

  • Category I: minimum basic salary RM10,000
  • Category II: RM5,000–RM9,999
  • Category III: RM3,000–RM4,999

The Playbook states these revised thresholds take effect on 1 June 2026.

Playbook, p. 28

Professional Visit Pass

Short-term professional work

The Playbook describes the PVP as a temporary pass for specific professional purposes such as training, consultancy, project work or machine installation and stabilisation.

  • For foreign professionals working on behalf of an overseas company.
  • Up to 12 months and not extendable.
  • Does not provide long-term employment rights or immigration privileges.
Playbook, pp. 28–29

Investor Pass

Business travellers

Introduced in 2025, the Playbook describes the Investor Pass as a multi-entry facility under the Social Visit Pass for business travellers.

  • Stay for up to 12 months: six months at a time, with an option to extend for a further six months, subject to justification and requirements.
  • Categories: New Investors, Investors in the Pipeline and Existing Investors.
Playbook, p. 29

Pass details and salary thresholds are reproduced from the Playbook, pp. 28–29. The Playbook refers readers to the relevant Malaysian agencies for application details.
